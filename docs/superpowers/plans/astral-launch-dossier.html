Three things prompted this dossier: the game reads as slop despite real engineering under the hood, a lot of it is too small to see or read, and — the sharpest finding, which came from you, not me — nobody who doesn't already know an auto-battler can learn to play this one. That third thing turned out to be the biggest lever. Everything below is ordered so you fix the thing that loses players first.

Part I

Cold Intelligence Report

What actually happens when someone with zero auto-battler knowledge opens the link, reconstructed from playing it and reading the code that drives each screen.

I played this cold across several full matches this session, then went back through App.tsx's phase machine and every modal it mounts to confirm what a first-timer can and can't discover on their own. The finding: there is no tutorial, no onboarding, and almost no in-context explanation anywhere in the game. A grep for "tutorial" across the entire src/ tree returns zero matches. Here is exactly what that produces, screen by screen.

Login → Hero Select

You type a callsign, pick a title, and are shown eight commanders with dense passive/active ability text (e.g. "Passive: Whenever a friendly Beast dies in combat, your remaining Beasts gain +1/+1 permanently"). Nothing tells you what a tribe is, why Beast synergy matters, or what playstyle each commander actually feels like to play. You pick blind, based on ability text you can't yet contextualize.

The Tavern

You land on "Astral Atrium Lobby," Turn 1, a countdown timer running, three cards for sale, and a "Tavern Tier" panel. The entire body of contextual help in this screen — and in the whole game — is one sentence: "Tips: Drag cards to position attack order (leftmost strikes first)." That's it. Nothing explains:

  • What Cog-Coins are or that they refresh each turn
  • What "Tavern Tier" does or why you'd spend coins upgrading it instead of buying minions
  • What "Freeze" does (it's a padlock icon with no label)
  • What "Reroll" costs or refreshes
  • That there even is a shared board other players are also building against

Keyword tooltips for BASTION, AETHER_BARRIER, LAST_GASP, MIASMIC, OVERCLOCK, RE_WIND, SWEEP, and MAGNETIC exist — but only inside CardInspectorModal, reached by hovering a card and clicking a small zoom glyph in its corner. A first-timer under a 70-second turn timer has no reason to discover that glyph exists.

Combat

You click "Engage Combat" and then nothing is asked of you again. Minions auto-fight in an order you may not have realized you set. Tribe signature attacks fire — a beast breathes fire, a machine fires a laser — with zero prior explanation that tribes even have signature attacks, or that this is a payoff for the synergy you might have stumbled into. The recently-verified PixiJS bloom and GSAP choreography looks good, but it's spectacle a new player can't yet read as meaningful.

The lore that already exists and nobody sees

There's a genuinely substantial world bible already written — src/engine/lore.ts defines realms, factions, a timeline, and named relics, surfaced through AstralCodexModal behind a small top-right icon. Nothing in the actual game ever points a new player at it, gives them a reason to open it, or frames why they're standing in a tavern recruiting monsters in the first place. It's a finished book nobody is handed.

The pattern across every screen is the same: the mechanical depth is real, the lore is real, the keyword system is real — but all three are opt-in, discoverable only by someone who already suspects they exist. A first-timer experiences none of it. That's the actual "AI slop" feeling: not that anything looks fake, but that nothing explains itself, which reads as unfinished regardless of polish.
Part II

The Slop Audit

Applying the removal test — name it, state its job, decide keep, fix, or cut — to the three things you flagged, plus one I found.

Body typeface: RajdhaniFix

Its job: render every stat, card name, tooltip, and button label in the game.

What I checked: it isn't a loading bug. index.html correctly pulls Rajdhani from Google Fonts, and index.css sets body { font-family: 'Rajdhani', sans-serif } as a base rule. It's genuinely rendering.

Why it still reads as generic: Rajdhani is one of the single most reused typefaces in dark-fantasy and sci-fi "gamer UI" template kits — it's a reflex pick for exactly this aesthetic, which is precisely what makes a choice slop under the no-ai-design-slop test even when correctly implemented: it "could be pasted into an unrelated product unchanged." Cinzel for headings is fine and untouched — you named body text specifically, and that's the half that's the problem.

Fix: new body pairing in Part III, one considered swap, not a redesign.

Motion: two competing languagesFix

Its job: give clicks and hovers feedback.

What I checked: combat now runs on hand-tuned GSAP timelines with real easing (elastic.out(1.2, 0.4), power2.out) from the recent VFX work — that part is good and shouldn't be touched. Everywhere else, transition-all appears 49 times across the component tree, almost always paired with the same hover:scale-105 active:scale-95 combo copy-pasted button to button, menu to menu.

Why it reads as dated: transition-all animates every property indiscriminately (including layout-triggering ones) and Tailwind's default ease is famously flat — the seam between "someone hand-tuned this" (combat) and "this is a template default" (everything else) is exactly where the game feels unfinished, because you can feel the two hands.

Fix: one shared GSAP micro-interaction layer reusing combat's own easing curves, in Part III.

Readability & scaleFix

Its job: let a player read what a card does before deciding to buy it.

What I checked, in CardView.tsx: card name renders at text-[11px], ability description at text-[10px], tribe tag at text-[8px], cost/attack badges at text-[9px]–text-[10px] — on a shop card that is w-28, 112 pixels wide. That's below comfortable reading size on a desktop monitor, and this is the size real shared-link traffic will hit first on a phone.

Fix: real minimum sizes and a slightly larger base card, in Part III.

Unit art: 40 cards, 6 picturesFix — top priority

Its job: make each of the 40 minions in src/engine/cards.ts feel like a distinct creature worth recruiting.

What I checked: every single card falls back to card.artUrl || TRIBE_ART_MAP[card.tribe] — and not one of the 40 cards actually sets its own artUrl. There are 6 tribes and 6 pieces of art. Every Automata card, from a 1-cost Cogwork Scrapper to a top-tier Clockwork Overlord, shows the identical image. This is the single most concrete, fixable reason the game reads as unfinished, and it's exactly the gap Gemini is well suited to close — see Part VI.

Motion: one signature, not two

Replace the 49 transition-all instances with two small shared GSAP helpers — a hover-lift and a press-settle — using the easing already proven in CombatArena3D.tsx (power2.out for lifts, elastic.out(1.2, 0.4) for anything that should feel physical, like the reroll lever or freeze padlock from the prior VFX spec). No new animation library: GSAP is already a dependency and already the house style in combat. Adding Framer Motion or a second engine here would just recreate the two-language problem this is meant to fix.

Where a second library genuinely earns its place

You asked about Lenis specifically. I looked it up: Lenis is a momentum smooth-scroll library that pairs with GSAP's ScrollTrigger, and its own maintainers explicitly advise skipping it on data-heavy, non-scrolling interfaces — which describes most of this game (body is overflow-hidden h-screen w-screen, there's no page scroll to smooth). Adding it app-wide would be exactly the kind of reflexive library addition the slop framework warns against. It does have one honest home: AstralCodexModal.tsx and Leaderboard.tsx are the only two components with real overflow-y-auto scrolling, and the Codex in particular is meant to feel like paging through an in-world book — that's the "scroll feel is a differentiator" case Lenis is actually for. Scope it to those two panels only.

Also worth knowing about for the icon-level animations from the prior spec (the mechanical freeze padlock, the spinning reroll lever, gear cogs): Rive or Lottie (via @lottiefiles/dotlottie-react) produce tiny, production-quality looping vector animations that are easier to art-direct precisely than hand-written CSS keyframes. Optional, not required — only reach for one if Antigravity's CSS version of those icons looks stiff after Part VIII lands.

Sources checked for this section Lenis/GSAP scope guidance and the "skip it for data-heavy UI" rule, plus Rive/Lottie as current production options for vector icon motion — both confirmed via live web search this session, not from training memory.
Part IV

The First Match

A mandatory, skippable-per-step guided walkthrough built on the existing phase state machine — the way Hearthstone Battlegrounds actually teaches itself.

Mechanism: a thin overlay component (TutorialOverlay.tsx) that spotlights one real UI element at a time with a short callout, gated by a localStorage flag (aabg_tutorial_seen) so it only forces itself on genuinely new players and never again. Every step has a visible "Skip tutorial" link — anyone who already knows the genre gets out in one click, on step one.

Part V

Card Clarity Before You Buy

The other half of your onboarding complaint: seeing what a card actually does before spending a coin on it, under a running turn timer.

The full CardInspectorModal already exists and is good — but it's a click-to-open modal, which is the wrong interaction for a 70-second shopping timer. Add a lighter hover preview: after ~350ms of hovering any shop or hand card, a floating panel appears beside it (never covering the card itself) showing full-size art, full-size ability text, and each keyword rendered with its one-line explanation inline — reusing the existing KEYWORD_EXPLANATIONS map from CardInspectorModal.tsx directly rather than duplicating it. No click, no modal, no interrupted flow. The full inspector modal stays for a deliberate, unhurried look (hero select, post-match review).

Part VII

Google Flow Cinematic Orders

Video earns three spots: the title screen, the eight hero-select portraits, and short mood beats inside the new tutorial. Not a UI explainer — Flow renders atmosphere and character, not diagrams.

A scope call, stated plainly Flow is very good at cinematic atmosphere and bad at literally explaining a "click here" UI interaction. The tutorial's actual teaching happens through the overlay copy in Part IV, not video. Flow's job in the tutorial is a short ambient establishing shot playing behind Step 0's lore hook — mood, not instruction.
Character consistency: use Ingredients to Video, not text alone Every hero and the title archway already exist as painted key art. Don't re-describe them from scratch in a text prompt — that lets the model reinterpret the face, outfit, and palette on every generation. Instead, in Flow, select Video Ingredients and drag in the existing asset itself as a reference (up to 3 per generation), then write a prompt that only describes motion — the ingredient supplies appearance. That's what every order below now assumes; each names the exact file to upload. One caveat straight from Google's own guidance: ingredient references work best on a plain or segmented background, and these portraits are full painted scenes, so if a first pass drifts on likeness, isolate the character onto a clean background with Gemini/Nano Banana first, then feed that into Flow as the ingredient.

Set once: Agent Instructions

Flow's Agent has a project-scoped, persistent instruction set (enable the Agent, then click the Agent Instructions icon in the prompt box → Add instruction) — it takes one reference image plus free-text guidelines and applies them to every generation in the project, so the tone/lighting/palette rules below don't need repeating in each of the 9 orders. Set this up once, first, before running anything else in this part.

Agent Instructions — Set Once Per Project
This project generates short cinematic video assets for "Aetherium: Astral Battlegrounds," a dark astral-steampunk auto-battler card game. Maintain one consistent visual world across every generation. Tone: moody, painterly dark-fantasy key art. Cosmic void backdrops, volumetric light, drifting embers and astral dust. Never bright, cartoonish, or comedic. Lighting: one dramatic rim-light source per shot, cool base tones (teal/violet/indigo) with warm gold/brass accents on metal, runes, and light sources. Never flat or evenly lit. Motion: slow and deliberate, always seamlessly loopable. No fast cuts, no shake, no rapid zooms. Framing: character/hero clips are vertical 9:16, subject centered with generous headroom. Environment/establishing shots are 16:9. Never render on-screen text, logos, watermarks, subtitles, or UI elements of any kind — these are pure key art; all text is added later in the game engine. When a reference image is supplied as an ingredient for a specific character, preserve their exact face, outfit, and coloring precisely. Only animate the scene and environment around them — never reinterpret or restyle the character itself. Tribe color law, when a character or scene belongs to one of these six factions: Automata = gunmetal steel + brass + cyan glow. Voidborn = obsidian violet + magenta-black glow. Alchemist = emerald + violet + acid-yellow glow. Celestial = gold + starlight blue + ivory. Beast = crimson + burnt umber + bone white. Pirate = weathered teal-cyan + tarnished gold.

The Antigravity Build Spec

Antigravity Build Spec — Onboarding & Polish Pass
### Context Aetherium: Astral Battlegrounds (React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind, Vite, PixiJS+GSAP combat VFX already shipped and verified) reads as unfinished for two separate reasons: (1) generic reflexive choices — body font Rajdhani, 49 instances of Tailwind `transition-all` + `hover:scale-105`, card text as small as 8px — and (2) a complete absence of onboarding: zero tutorial, keyword tooltips buried in a rarely-discovered modal, and 40 unique cards sharing only 6 pieces of tribe-level art. ### Constraints - Preserve the existing dark astral-steampunk identity and the Cinzel heading typeface exactly — this is refinement, not redesign. - Do not introduce a second animation engine (no Framer Motion, no competing scroll libraries) where GSAP already covers the case — consolidate onto GSAP's existing easing vocabulary (`power2.out`, `elastic.out(1.2, 0.4)`) from CombatArena3D.tsx. - `npx vitest run` and `npm run build` must pass clean before and after, same bar as the VFX pass. - New video assets must degrade gracefully to the existing static art if a file is missing or fails to load — never a blank screen. ### Phase 1 - Typography system Swap `tailwind.config.js` `fontFamily.sans` from Rajdhani to Familjen Grotesk; add a `mono: ['JetBrains Mono', 'monospace']` entry. Update the Google Fonts link in `index.html` and the base `body` rule in `index.css`. Raise minimum card text sizes in `CardView.tsx`: name to 14px, ability text to 13px, tribe/cost badges to 12px; widen the `sm` card size token from `w-28` to a 140px-wide equivalent. Route numeric stat displays (attack, health, cost, damage numbers) through the new mono face. ### Phase 2 - Motion consolidation Replace the 49 `transition-all` + `hover:scale-105`/`active:scale-95` instances across `TavernShop.tsx`, `HomePageModal.tsx`, `HeroSelectModal.tsx`, and any other component matching that pattern with two small shared GSAP helpers (a hover-lift using `power2.out`, a press-settle using `elastic.out(1.2, 0.4)`) so DOM chrome motion matches the physicality already established in combat. Scope Lenis smooth-scroll narrowly to the two components with real internal scrolling, `AstralCodexModal.tsx` and `Leaderboard.tsx` only — do not add it app-wide, since the app shell itself has no page scroll to smooth. ### Phase 3 - Card clarity: hover preview Add a hover-triggered hover preview panel (delay ~350ms) reusable from any shop or hand card: full-size art, full ability text, and every keyword the card has rendered inline with its explanation, sourced directly from the existing `KEYWORD_EXPLANATIONS` map in `CardInspectorModal.tsx` (do not duplicate that data).
